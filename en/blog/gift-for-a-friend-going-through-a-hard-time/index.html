Gifts for a friend going through a hard time: 9 no-pressure ideas

27 November 2026 · 6 min read

When a friend is struggling, you want to do something but don't know what. Words sound fake, "hang in there" doesn't help, and a big gesture can feel overwhelming. A gift works like a note without words: "I'm thinking of you, I expect nothing from you". The key is that it shouldn't demand energy they don't have right now.

We're not therapists. This list is about care, not treatment. Depression is treated by professionals; a gift simply reminds someone they're not alone.

9 gift ideas for a friend who's having a hard time

1. Food they don't have to cook

When energy runs out, proper meals are the first thing to go. A delivery of their favourite dish, soup in containers for a few days or a voucher for a café near home takes one daily task off their plate.

2. Help with chores

A paid cleaning session, laundry, or simply "I'll come over on Saturday and do the dishes". It sounds mundane, but for someone with depression a pile of dishes can feel like a real mountain.

3. Something soft and warm

A throw, warm socks, an oversized hoodie. Things you can wrap yourself in give a sense of safety without any effort.

4. A handwritten note or letter

A few honest sentences: what you value about your friend, which shared memory still makes you laugh. No "everything will be fine". People keep notes like that for years.

5. Time together, no obligations

"I'll be around on Sunday, we can watch a show or just sit in silence." Make sure they can say no without feeling guilty.

6. A houseplant that's hard to kill

A snake plant or a ZZ plant will survive a week without water. Something green and alive quietly brightens a room without becoming one more obligation.

7. An audiobook or podcast instead of a paper book

Reading is hard when concentration is low. Listening can be done lying down, with no effort at all.

8. A painting that makes them smile

We make gifts like this ourselves, so here's honestly why they work. An artist turns your friend's sad photo into a kind caricature painted on canvas: the situation becomes funny, never the person. The painting stays on the wall and quietly reminds them that someone sees how it is and laughs with them, not at them. See what it looks like on our home page.

9. A paid session with a professional, only if they want it

If your friend has said they'd like to see a therapist but keeps putting it off because of money, help with the cost can be the most valuable gift. Don't make it a surprise and don't push.

What not to give

  • "How to be happy" books and motivational posters. They read like a reproach.
  • A gym membership with a hint that "you need to move more". One more obligation they won't keep and will blame themselves for.
  • Big surprises and parties. A crowd's attention can be exhausting.
  • Alcohol. It makes things worse and doesn't mix well with medication.

How to give it gently

  • No expectations. Don't wait for a joyful reaction or a long thank you. A quiet "thanks" is already a lot.
  • No conditions. It's a gift, not a trade for "now pull yourself together".
  • In private. Not in front of everyone, so they can react however they can right now.
  • And stay close afterwards. A gift opens a conversation, it doesn't replace it.
