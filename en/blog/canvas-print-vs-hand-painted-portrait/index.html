Portrait from a photo: canvas print vs hand-painted — what's the difference

27 November 2026 · 5 min read

Search for a "portrait from photo on canvas" and you'll see hundreds of offers at very different prices. The reason is usually not greedy artists: two different products are sold under one name, printing and painting.

In short: how they differ

Photo printed on canvasHand-painted portrait
How it's madeThe photo is edited and printed onto fabricAn artist paints the picture from scratch, looking at the photo
SurfaceFlat, like a photographVisible strokes, paint texture, brush marks
UniquenessAs many copies as you likeOne single painting
TimeUsually 1–2 daysSeveral days of an artist's work
PriceSeveral times cheaperMore expensive: hours of handwork
Can the scene changeOnly filters and collageYes: add details, a joke, a new background

When to choose a print

A print is honest and useful when you want the photograph itself, large: a wedding photo for the wall, a landscape from a trip, a family picture for grandma. Fast, affordable, exactly like the photo.

When to choose a painting

A painting is for when the artist's view matters more than an exact copy: adding, exaggerating, joking, capturing a mood. Such a portrait is seen as art and a gift that lasts for years, not a photo in a frame. That's how we work at Sour Mugs: a portrait from a photo or a caricature from a photo is painted by hand on canvas.

How not to buy a print sold as a painting

  • Look for "print", "giclée", "printed canvas" or "your photo on canvas" in the description. That's almost always a printer.
  • Ask for a photo of the process. An artist can easily show a half-finished canvas or a palette.
  • Zoom in on examples: in a painting you can see strokes and uneven paint.
  • Watch out for "oil painting effect". Often it's a digital filter printed on canvas.
  • Compare price and timing. A large "oil painting" for the price of a takeaway, ready in a day, is unlikely to be painted by hand.

A special case: a digital portrait drawn on a tablet. A person makes it, but the result is printed. It's a nice option if you want a stylised picture, not a painting.
